Most leagues send their old heroes into retirement. The Paradice League hands them a price tag. A good number of the Star Mercenaries you can hire in our league were regular players in seasons gone by, and every coach who faces one knows exactly where he came from. Some of them helped build those legends. Some of them were on the receiving end.

## How a player gets a price tag

Nobody knows exactly where the decisions are made. Some say it happens in a back room behind the old stadium, others swear it's a candlelit cellar that only exists on the night before a new season. What everyone agrees on: there is a jury. Nobody has ever seen their faces. Nobody admits to being on it. And every season, a few old players suddenly find a price tag around their necks.

The jury is said to look for two things. A sensible package of skills, earned on the pitch. And one thing that sticks out. A trait that drags his Agility down. A skill nobody would expect on his position. Something that makes coaches stop and say "wait, he has *what*?"

Then the hooded figures argue, vote and veto anyone who would tip the balance too far. A mercenary who wins matches on his own is bad for business. One who makes them interesting is very good for it.

Here are three of them.

In 2024, Beelzebub was more than a player for Lewdgrip's Beelzebubis. He was the poster boy, the mascot and the reason the team had its name. He delivered too: 3 casualties and 2 MVPs, all the way to the playoffs, where Coach Smaug's Don't Loose Your Head ended the run.

The unusual bit? Claws. Trolls usually just hit things very hard. Beelzebub hits things very hard and makes thick armour a lot less useful. Add Guard and you have a troll who is useful even when he forgets what he was doing.

2025 was quiet. Nobody called. Then came the 2026 playoff quarter-final between Umberto's Pop Khorne and Outcaarst's Seuchenkontrolle, and suddenly everyone called. Both coaches negotiated with his manager. Unfortunately for them, the manager was also a Chaos Sorcerer. He cast a little spell, duplicated his client and cashed in on both sides.

Turn one: two Beelzebubs, two casualties. One each. The manager has not been seen without a smile since.

## Connor McLeod: one clansman, two heads

Coach Smaug's Don't Loose Your Head came from the rainy highlands, a band of Khorne-worshipping clans who settle every argument with a blade. Connor McLeod was the sharpest of them. In 2024 he put 8 opponents off the pitch. Not the most brutal player of the season, but close to the top of the list.

Block and Mighty Blow he earned the hard way, match after match and training session after training session. The second head was a different story. That one was a gift from the Blood God himself, presumably as thanks for all the skulls. Two Heads is not what you expect on a Bloodseeker, and it makes him a lot harder to pin down than one should be.

After a quiet 2025, it was Umberto who came knocking in 2026, ahead of the semi-final against Smaug's new team, the Dark Elves of Morathis Murders. The plan was simple and slightly cruel: send Connor against the coach who trained him. Connor knew every trick Smaug had taught him, and as a devoted follower of the Blood God he had good reason to expect some divine goodwill wearing Umberto's colours.

It worked. Pop Khorne won 2:0, with a good share of the credit going to inside information from a two-headed clansman.

## David Hasselhoff: a man of many talents

Before he ever wore a helmet, David had already lived several lives. He sang as a bard in market squares. He worked as a lifeguard on the Reik. He drove coaches for the famous Kreutzhofen Innung Transport and Transfer, better known as K.I.T.T. And if the stories are true, he once sang so beautifully that the feuding districts of Averheim West and Averheim East tore down the wall between them and chased their mayor out of town.

In 2024 he added Blitzer to the list, trained by Coach Schief for the Old World Alliance side Old Men Alliance. A solid season: 3 touchdowns, 5 casualties, 1 MVP. It ended in the last group game with a loss to Umberto's Underworld Denizens, Notes Paradice 24. Fifth place. Playoffs missed by a whisker.

Retirement? Not for David. He offered his services as a mercenary from 2025 on and was hired five times in his first season. Guard and Tackle make him useful anywhere on the pitch. Pro makes him the most professional man in any dugout he walks into, which is exactly what you'd expect from someone with this CV.

In 2026, though, nobody has called. Not once. Coaches, he's still waiting by the phone.

## The man behind the money

Look closely and one name keeps turning up. Umberto's Underworld Denizens ended David's playing days in 2024. He hired a Beelzebub in the 2026 quarter-final. He hired Connor McLeod for the semi-final and won it.

On Tuesday, December 1, Umberto plays the Season 5 final against Xurpils' Back to the Roots. We have no idea who he'll bring. But if a two-headed clansman or a slightly confused troll walks out of the Pop Khorne dugout, you heard it here first.
