Some players win you games with touchdowns. Some win them with casualties. And then there is the Flesh Golem, who wins them by standing on a square and refusing to leave it.

My latest outing on FUMBBL was nothing to frame. My Necromantic Horror side, the [Carpathian Nighthowls](https://fumbbl.com/t/1316933), drew 1:1 with a Khorne team in a game that never quite found its rhythm. But one thing stuck with me after the final whistle. Their Bloodspawn, Strength 5, Claws, Mighty Blow, the full menu, spent the whole match at the sideline, trying to shove my golem into the crowd and getting nowhere, while the ball was somewhere far away. Not because he was hurt. Because a Flesh Golem with Block was standing in front of him and simply would not go away.

So this one is for the golem.

## Built from spare parts, built to last

On paper the Flesh Golem is not a poster boy. Movement 4, Strength 4, the agility of a wardrobe. What makes him special is the bundle he comes with: Armour 10+, Regeneration, Thick Skull and Stand Firm. He is hard to break, hard to keep down, and even when he does end up in the casualty box, Regeneration gives the flesh-knitters a decent chance to have him back on the bench before the next drive.

Then you add Block. The Both Down that used to put him on his back becomes a shrug, and the golem turns into what every bash coach dreams of: a player the opponent has to deal with, but can neither move nor remove.

## The art of letting go (once)

Stand Firm is optional. The golem may refuse to be pushed back, but he does not have to. That small word "may" is where the fun starts against Frenzy.

A Frenzy player who pushes his target and finds him still standing has to follow up and hit again. So when the Bloodspawn came in, my golem let the first push happen. One square back, towards the sideline and towards my own lines. The Bloodspawn followed, as Frenzy demands. On the second block, the golem planted his feet and used Stand Firm. The Bloodspawn now stood one square deeper than he wanted to, next to the touchline and right in front of a few of my players who had been waiting for exactly that.

That is the classic Frenzy trap, with a golem as the bait. And the numbers flip fast. Bloodspawn Strength 5 against golem Strength 4 means two dice for the Khorne coach. Next turn the golem hits back with one assist: 5 against 5, one die. With two assists: 6 against 5, two dice, and the golem picks. The big red monster went from hunter to hunted in a single turn.

And the rest of the match? The golem kept him in his tackle zone. The Bloodspawn could block him, but with Stand Firm there was no push, no follow-up and no way through. He could try to dodge away, but that is a gamble most Khorne coaches would rather not take with their most expensive player. So he stayed. A Strength 5 killer, gift-wrapped and parked at the sideline.

## Part of a machine

One golem is a wall. Golems with friends are a fortress. With Necromantic Horror the real magic is the teamwork with the Wraiths, especially once they have picked up Guard. Wraiths bring Block, Sidestep and Foul Appearance, golems with Block refuse to fall over, and together they own the middle of the pitch. The opponent gets pushed, nudged and funnelled into a few unpleasant lanes. Waiting at the end of those lanes: the Werewolves, who are always happy to see you. And the zombies shuffling around in between plug the remaining gaps simply by being in the way.

The golem's job in that machine is not glamorous. He holds a cage corner, he anchors a sideline, he breaks a push chain. He rarely scores and rarely gets the MVP. He does not care. He is a golem.

## Who he loves to meet

- **Frenzy teams.** Khorne, or any player who has to keep hitting. Every block they throw at him is a block they do not throw at someone squishier, and the optional Stand Firm lets you decide where they end up.
- **Sideline bullies.** Crowd surfing needs a push. No push, no surf.
- **Push-chain artists.** Coaches who like to shove a whole line around run into a cornerstone that does not move.

## Who ruins his day

- **Juggernaut.** On a Blitz, Juggernaut switches off Stand Firm. A Minotaur or Rat Ogre who rolled Juggernaut, or a Saurus, who comes with it built in under the 2025 rules, can move him wherever they like. Khorngors carry it too, so even on Khorne day the golem is not entirely safe. I have been on the wrong end of most of these.
- **Hypnotic Gaze.** A Vampire looks deep into his mismatched eyes, and for a moment the golem is just very large, very confused furniture: no tackle zone, no skills, no Stand Firm. Suddenly the cage has a door.
- **Raw muscle.** Strength 4 is respectable, not exceptional. Big Guys with a couple of assists can still put him on the turf.

## Frank 'n' Stein: the original

No homage without the most famous Flesh Golem of all. Frank has been around for a very long time, and his paperwork has changed almost as often as his limbs.

**The second edition version.** In the second edition of the game, back in the late eighties, his star player card lists him as Frank N. Stein, number 11, blocker for the Bruendar Grimjacks. Race: Human. Alignment: Chaos. Age: 100+. He had MA 4, ST 4 and AV 10, plus Mighty Blow (level 2) and Toughness (level 3), which made him almost impossible to hurt, unless the dice came up 12, in which case he was simply dead. No Stand Firm back then. That skill did not exist yet.

His career stats on that old card are a joy: 17 touchdowns, 75 player fatalities, 53 spectator fatalities, one coach fatality and, for a Flesh Golem, a frankly suspicious 47 interceptions, returned for 916 paces. Somebody give that man a Catch skill.

**The modern version.** When Frank returned under the 2020 rules, the writers built a whole origin story around those old numbers. During the Grimjacks' 2489 tour of Sylvania, a squad of rookies and leftovers ran into a Necromancer with ambitions. Two unremarkable blockers, Franklyn Smyth and Steiner S. Scottward, were cut apart and stitched back together as one. Franklyn had the tactical brain but no grip on the ball. Steiner had the size and most of the limbs, but very little upstairs. Together they became a star: Player of the Year for the Grimjacks in his first season.

Then came 2494. Chaos Cup, opening round, Grimjacks 2-0 up against the Blitzburg Stealers, when a freak lightning storm struck Frank mid-drive. Instead of frying him, the jolt sent him into a blind rage. The referee, his own head coach and 53 Stealers supporters did not survive it. Spot the numbers? The 53 spectators and the one coach from the 1988 card made it into the new legend. The match was handed to the Stealers, Frank was chased out of the stadium by fans of both teams and his contract was torn up.

Four years later he resurfaced on Cabalvision, starring in a commercial for Lightningade Electrolyte Drink that used footage of the massacre to show how much energy the drink gives you. Questionable taste, tenfold sales. A fan campaign brought him back to the pitch, and today he plays for anyone who can afford his wages, the Blitzburg Stealers included. They have forgiven him. It happens.

His trophy cabinet deserves a mention too: Best Newcomer, a Services to Organ Transplants Medal, Most Limbs Severed and, inevitably, Lightningade's Most Shocking Moment Award. Somewhere along the way he also went from 230 lbs to 442 lbs, which is roughly what you get when you add a second person.

Under the 2020 rules he came back with a new model and these numbers:

- **Fee:** 250,000 gold
- **Stats:** MA 4, ST 5, AG 4+, AV 10+
- **Skills:** Break Tackle, Loner (4+), Mighty Blow (+1), Regeneration, Stand Firm, Thick Skull
- **Special rule, Brutal Block:** once per game, +1 to an Injury roll after a Block, even after the dice have been rolled
- **Plays for:** Old World Classic and Sylvanian Spotlight teams

In the 2025 rules he has not made the Star Player list. Yet, we hope.

My own Frank is the old metal miniature from the second edition, bought while completing my collection of classic metal figures. I like him because he is properly old. And here is the twist: on the tabletop he never plays as a Star Player. Whenever I bring Necromantic Horror, he takes the field as a perfectly normal Flesh Golem. Which means the most famous golem in history was on the pitch when I won the Palinkabowl 2024 in Budapest, quietly doing golem things. No Brutal Block, no Mighty Blow. Just standing there.

Exactly as it should be.
