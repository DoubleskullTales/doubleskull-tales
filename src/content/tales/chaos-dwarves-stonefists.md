My first team was a box of Chaos Dwarves from the comic shop in my hometown. That was 1996. I've coached a lot of teams since, but if you asked me which one I'd save from a burning dugout, it's still the little guys with the big hats.

So when the new Chaos Dwarf roster came out, I was nervous. I wasn't the only one.

## What changed

The new roster shook up a recipe that had worked for decades:

- Only four Blockers, and none of them start with Tackle anymore
- Two Flamesmiths who can breathe fire
- Hobgoblins lost their access to General skills
- In return, two Sneaky Stabbas joined the team

On its own, that's not a bad idea. Chaos Dwarves had played the same way for a very long time, and a bit of variety does any team good. But on paper it looked like they'd lost some teeth.

I tested them a few times. I didn't hate them. I just didn't find the old fun again.

Then something clicked.

## Who does the work

**The Minotaur.** Break Tackle, Two Heads and Claws. That's the best thing on the roster right now. Break Tackle and Two Heads mean no cage stays closed for long. Claws means no armour stays safe either. After ten games: 9 casualties, 1 touchdown and 2 MVPs.

**The Bull Centaurs.** They're the scoring machine. Between them, the two Bulls scored 15 of the team's 21 touchdowns. Break Tackle still works great on them. Two Heads, not so much. One of mine has it, and he scores about as often as the other one (7 touchdowns against 8). He just costs more. As a secondary skill it makes a Bull too expensive for what you get back.

**The Blockers.** Only four of them now, but Iron Hard Skin makes them perfect for the line of scrimmage. Mighty Blow and Claws lose their bite, so I can put them on the line without worrying. And they still hit back: 16 casualties between the four of them. One Blocker with Mighty Blow has 9, exactly as many as the Minotaur. He lost a point of Agility along the way. Nobody on this team was using it anyway.

**The Sneaky Stabbas.** At 60k each, they're a bargain. With Violent Innovator they collect SPP fast. One of mine has played five games and already has 4 casualties, 2 touchdowns and 14 SPP. Three of those casualties came by stabbing in one game against Snotlings. The Snotlings were not impressed. The other Stabba is still waiting for his first SPP. We're working on it.

**The Flamesmiths.** I wanted to like them. I really did. But hoping for a 6 to breathe fire, and then usually watching him fall over in the process, gets old quickly. Mine simply block and let Brawler do the work. Less spectacular, much more reliable. The numbers agree: my regular Flamesmith has 2 casualties and no MVP in ten games.

## The numbers

Ten games with the Zharr Bzharr Stonefists on FUMBBL:

- **Record:** 8 wins, 2 draws, 0 losses
- **Touchdowns:** 21 scored, 5 conceded (2.1 per game)
- **Casualties:** 43 caused, 12 suffered (4.3 per game)

Yes, a few of those games were against Stunties and Elves. I won't pretend otherwise. But you know me. Bashing is just more fun.

## The price of success

Winning has a cost. The team value is already huge, and at that level the only teams that want to play you are the heavy ones. Nurgle. Khorne. Teams that hit back.

The good news: Chaos Dwarves can take it. And they're more than happy to hand it out.

## Verdict

I was skeptical. I was wrong. The new Chaos Dwarves play differently, and it took me a while to figure out how. Less grinding with Tackle, more breaking cages with the Minotaur and letting the Stabbas do the dirty work.

Thirty years after that box from the comic shop, they're fun again. Old love, new tricks.
